# Question guide

Each hundo question asks players to predict the room, not to recall a fact or state their own preference. There is no fixed correct answer: the leading choice is calculated from eligible players' votes when the timer closes.

For every daily set of ten, cover what the room might buy, sell or hold; what it fears; what it trusts or believes; what it expects next; and how it might act. A theme of the day can tie the set together, but it should not replace that range. The result should be interesting to review even for people who were eliminated.

Use short, plain international English that can be read with four distinct options in a 15-second question window. Avoid culture-specific jokes, local times of day, overlapping options and answers that can be looked up. Put easier-to-read scenarios first; review the wording on a phone before scheduling. A question is worth keeping when its result would be interesting from a large room of crypto players.

These votes measure players' **expectations about the room**. They are not a direct survey of each player's own holdings, intentions or beliefs. Describe the public recap as what the room predicted or chose, and do not claim that a sample game contains real votes.

Future question packs remain private until their rounds finish. Before each scheduled week, check that all ten questions still make sense in the current market and replace stale wording. Rehearsal rounds have no payout, regardless of any prototype prize shown in the app.
